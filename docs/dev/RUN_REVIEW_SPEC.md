# VNP Combat Prototype: Run Review

Automated playtesting (`docs/dev/AUTOMATED_PLAYTESTING_SPEC.md`) gave the game pilots that play it and batches that summarise hundreds of fights. Getting pilots to play like the designer then meant the designer watching pilot fights and reporting what looked wrong, with clock times, and Claude replaying those moments to see why. That fixed real problems, but each round cost the designer a watched fight, and the gains became too small to measure.

This spec takes over most of that watching. The game notices the kinds of moments the designer kept reporting, such as flying into a Goo, and records them in every fight, the designer's own included. Beyond those known moments, the goal is for Claude to review fights visually on its own and notice problems nobody has described yet. For that, every fight can be drawn as a top-down overview map of the whole fight, and any moment as a strip of small maps a second apart. Claude scans these, the way the designer watched, and confirms what it finds against the fight's numbers and the pilot's reasoning before reporting it. Automatic calibration, kept as an option in the automated playtesting spec, moves here, with the detector counts as extra targets.

It interrupts phase 3 (`docs/design/PROTOTYPE_3_SPEC.md`) again, at the designer's request, since it speeds up all tuning after it. Phase 3 resumes afterwards from its stage 3. Everything in the earlier specs still applies unless this file changes it.

## What this is for

- Can the moments the designer reported while watching pilots be found without anyone watching?
- How often does each happen to pilots, and how often to the designer?
- Can Claude notice problems on its own from maps of whole fights and moments, as the designer did by watching, including ones no detector looks for?
- Can pilots be calibrated to the designer by these counts as well as by results, without the designer watching?

## Terms

- **Detector:** a rule that watches a fight and notices one kind of moment, such as the VNP touching a Goo. It works from what happens in the fight, not from the pilot's reasoning, so it judges the designer's fights and a pilot's in the same way.
- **Flagged moment:** a time when a detector fired, with what it saw.
- **Track:** the VNP's path through a whole fight, recorded a few times a second, with what it was doing and the events along the way.
- **Overview map:** one top-down picture of a whole fight, drawn from its track.
- **Snapshot:** the state of the fight around the VNP at one moment: the enemies, drones, material and shots near it.
- **Strip map:** a row of small top-down pictures of snapshots about a second apart, in one image, so a moment's motion reads at a glance.
- **Maps** are plain diagrams, not the game's own look.

## Detectors

Each detector fires on something that can be seen in the fight, so it works the same on the designer's fights and on a pilot's. Its thresholds are in the review settings file, not in code. A detector that keeps firing on the same thing counts it once, until the moment has clearly passed.

Starting detectors, each from something the designer reported:

| Detector | Fires when | From |
|---|---|---|
| Touched a contact enemy | the VNP touches an enemy that damages by touch, such as a Goo | flew into a Goo (clock 4:06) |
| Flew through a group | the VNP passes within a short distance of several enemies within a few seconds | flew through a group to a cluster beyond it (clock 4:36) |
| Closed in on a near enemy | the VNP moves toward an enemy that is already close, for more than a moment | moved even closer to an enemy that was already close |
| Indecision | the VNP's direction reverses several times within a few seconds with enemies near | juggled between regrouping and fighting (clock 2:25) |
| Creeping | the VNP moves below most of its top speed for a while, while not harvesting and not easing in to harvest | slowed down between fights (clock 4:52, and earlier) |
| Fighting without drones | with no drones, the VNP stays within weapon reach of enemies for a while instead of getting away | kept taking shots with no drones (clock 3:06 and 2:15) |
| Idle | the VNP neither harvests nor has an enemy within reach for a while | pilots ending fights far from the gateway, noticed by Claude |
| Drones lost in a burst | many drones are absorbed or destroyed within a few seconds, and again each time a burst worsens to `BurstWorsens` times the count that fired it | drones locking onto one Goo and flying into others |
| Drones left fighting | several drones are fighting an enemy near them while the VNP, farther off, moves away | Claude's first review of a batch (stage 3) |
| Drones not rebuilt | the drones stay at a few or none for a while after the first minute | Claude's first review of a batch (stage 3) |

Detectors are a list, so more can be added as new kinds of moments come up. They judge enemies by what they do (absorbing drones, damaging by touch), never by which enemy they are, so they keep working as enemy types are added. Each counts how often it fired, per minute of fight, and keeps its flagged moments.

## Overview maps

One image per fight, for scanning whole fights for anything odd.

- **Track:** every fight records the VNP's position a few times a second, with what it was doing, judged from what can be seen so that it works for the designer's fights too: harvesting (its beam is working, or it or a weapon such as Orbital Shards harvested within `HarvestingWithin` seconds), fighting (an enemy within its weapons' reach), moving away from enemies, or travelling. Along the way it records events: hits taken, drones lost, Goo touches, level-ups, the kill target, each wave and escalation step, and each detector firing.
- **The map:** the whole path on one top-down image, coloured by what the VNP was doing, with the gateway, markers for the events, and the time at regular marks along the path. Underneath, strips show health, drones, level and speed over the fight, on the same time scale, with the 5:00 limit marked. It shows the shape of a fight: long detours, circling one spot, going back and forth, lingering at the edge of a fight, wandering far from material.
- **Which fights:** a batch draws overview maps for a few fights per combination, such as its best, its worst and a typical one; any fight's map can also be drawn from its log afterwards.

## Strip maps

A few small top-down pictures of one moment, about a second apart, side by side in one image.

- **Each picture:** the area around the VNP, about what the screen shows. The VNP and its recent path stand out; enemies are coloured by type and drawn at their size; Goos show their reach; drones are small dots; material is sized by mass, asteroids and debris told apart; shots are short lines in their direction of travel. A label gives the fight time, the HUD clock, health, drones and level.
- **Flagged moments:** every flagged moment gets a strip, from a few seconds before it to a little after. The fight keeps the last few seconds of snapshots as it goes, so a strip of what led up to a moment can be saved when a detector fires, in the designer's fights as well as pilots'.
- **Any moment of a pilot fight:** a pilot fight repeats exactly from its seed, so the scenario harness can replay it headless and draw a strip around any fight time, such as something Claude spotted on an overview map. Since the time is known before the replay, the strip's pictures are taken on its own schedule: one exactly at the moment, at any spacing, or across a stretch of the fight.
- **The end of a fight:** a strip still being taken when the VNP is destroyed ends with a picture of that moment.
- **Drawn by the game:** maps are drawn by the game's editor-only code with Godot's own image drawing, which works headless and needs nothing installed. Batch scripts stay Python-only.
- **How many:** a batch draws strips for a limited number of each detector's moments, picked to cover different fights, so a big batch doesn't write thousands of pictures.

## At the scale of the full game

The full game will have dozens of enemy types and hundreds of weapons, weapon combinations and upgrades. Nothing in the review may depend on a fixed list of them.

- **Built:** weapons' objects on maps, through a hook every weapon must implement (see Weapon effects on maps and Nothing left out of recordings, under Settled during the build); detectors work from enemy traits; fight logs carry each enemy type's name, colour and traits from its definition, and each detector's name; maps draw each enemy type in a colour of its own from the drawer's palette, marked by shape for what they do (striped for damaging by touch, an outer ring for absorbing drones), with a key to the types in each picture, and take detector names and letters from the log. A new enemy type or detector appears on maps with no change to the drawer.
- **Planned, for when content grows:**
  - **Coverage:** batches can't play every combination, so random starts sample them. The report gains a coverage view: which weapons, combinations and upgrades a batch tried, and how often, so gaps are visible.
  - **Outliers:** the report flags combinations and upgrades whose win rate or detector rates sit far from the rest. Those flags pick what Claude looks at closely, since hundreds of combinations can't each be reviewed by eye.

## Claude's review of a batch

- **What it looks at:** the overview maps of a few fights per combination, and the strips of flagged moments, then strips of anything else that looks wrong on an overview.
- **Confirming:** a busy image can be misread, so before reporting anything Claude checks it against the fight's numbers and, for a pilot fight, replays it with `explain` to see the pilot's reasoning.
- **What it reports:** what it noticed, with the fight, the HUD clock and its maps, whether it was confirmed, and what might be causing it, in the same way the designer's notes were turned into changes. A pattern seen often enough becomes a new detector.

## Screenshots, on request

Real screenshots stay available for when how something looks on screen matters, such as effects hiding a Goo, which a map can't show. The scenario harness already saves one with `shot=`; taking several around a moment is added only if a review needs it. A screenshot needs a real window, so one opens on the designer's computer.

## The designer's fights

- Detectors run in the designer's own fights in the editor, and their counts, flagged moments and track go into the fight log, as with pilots, so the designer's fights get overview maps and strips of their flagged moments, drawn on request from the log and the recording.
- A designer's fight can't be replayed, so it records the picture around the VNP all fight long instead, into a compressed file beside its log, and any moment of it can be drawn afterwards, flagged or not.
- This shows where the designer's play differs from the pilots': for example, if pilots touch Goos five times a minute and the designer once.

## Reports

- **Fight logs:** each detector's count and rate, its flagged moments with fight time and clock time, their snapshots (for a recorded fight, the designer's, only the moments' times: their pictures come from the recording), and the fight's track and events, including waves and escalation steps.
- **Batch report:** a Review section, in both the Markdown and the page with charts:
  - each detector's rate per minute, by combination, beside the designer's own rate from their logs on the same tuning;
  - for each detector, its flagged moments worth looking at, each with its strip and the command to replay it;
  - the overview maps it drew.
- **Checking a change:** a pilot or tuning change can be judged by whether it lowers the detector rates it was meant to, without anyone watching.

## Automatic calibration

Moved here from the automated playtesting spec, where it was kept as an option.

- **Targets from the designer's logs:** win rate, level at 5:00, time to the kill target, damage taken per minute, drones lost to Goos per minute, time spent holding still, and now each detector's rate, on the designer's own starting weapons. As built, the search scores by health, drones, kills, level and survival at the same fight times (`compare_to_designer.py`'s gap); detector rates are not among its targets yet (see Automatic calibration (stage 4), under Settled during the build).
- **Search:** a script tries values for the expert pilot's settings (such as caution, dodging, harvest weight, `PressureFall` and the regroup thresholds) as tuning variants, plays each candidate on those starting weapons, and keeps the closest match to the targets.
- **Running it:** unattended, overnight or in the background. Batches run together while iterating stay under a minute.
- **Data:** it gets more reliable as the designer's logs grow. A handful of fights is too few to set targets.
- **The designer's part:** playing normally, so the logs grow, and watching a pilot only when the numbers or a map suggest something odd.
- **Two training targets (planned, `docs/dev/PILOT_SPEC.md`):**
  - **Like the designer:** calibration toward the designer's results, as here, against their recorded Grey Goo fights. (Fitting the rebuilt pilot's weights to the recordings first is deferred, at the designer's choice.)
  - **As strong as possible:** the ceiling pilot, calibrated for results instead.

  Strips will also draw the danger shapes enemies declare, and the path the pilot chose.

## Keeping it out of builds

All of it is a testing aid: detectors, tracks, snapshots, recordings, maps and calibration. Code in the game project goes in `scripts/cheats/playtesting/`, behind `#if TOOLS`, under the same rule as the playtest cheats. The few hooks it needs on game classes outside that folder, such as each weapon's `CollectPlaced`, go inside `#if RECORDING`, defined only for the editor's Debug build (see Out of exported builds, under Settled during the build). An exported game has none of it.

## Tuning values

| Value | Where |
|---|---|
| Each detector's thresholds, such as distances, counts and how long something must last | the review settings file in `scripts/cheats/playtesting/` |
| How often the track records the VNP's position | the review settings file |
| How many pictures a strip has, how far apart, and how far before and after a moment it reaches | the review settings file |
| How many overview maps and strips a batch draws | the review settings file |
| Whether the designer's fights are recorded, how often (`RecordPlayerFights`, `RecordSpacing`), and whether their maps are drawn when the log is written (`DrawPlayerMaps`) | the review settings file |
| How long after the VNP last harvested it still counts as harvesting (`HarvestingWithin`) | the review settings file |

## Not yet decided

The designer has not settled these. Use the behavior listed, say that you did, and keep it easy to change.

- **Which fights and moments a batch draws:** overview maps of the best, worst and a typical fight per combination; strips of a few moments per detector, spread across different fights and combinations.
- **Calibration targets' weights:** every target counts equally until there is data to say otherwise.

For design questions this file does not answer, ask the designer rather than inventing a rule.

## Settled during the build

The prototype now does the following; any of it can still change.

- **Detectors (stage 1):** `scripts/cheats/playtesting/RunReview.cs`, run by the fight log every frame of every fight in the editor, the designer's and pilots' alike. Thresholds are in `review_settings.tres` beside it.
  - **What each watches:** only what happens in the fight: the VNP's position, velocity and health, enemies' positions, sizes and types, drones lost, and whether the VNP's harvest beam is working (`VnpHarvester.Beaming`, the first addition to game code; the later ones are listed under Out of exported builds). Distances are from enemies' edges, speeds a fraction of the VNP's top speed.
  - **Firing once:** each fires when its condition has held long enough, then waits until the condition has been false for `RearmSeconds` before it can fire again, so one long moment counts once.
  - **Output:** each firing prints a `REVIEW:` line with fight time, the HUD clock and what it saw, so a watched scenario shows them as they happen. The fight log's `review` section has each detector's count, rate per minute and moments, each with its fight time, HUD clock and a short description.
  - **Fights stay repeatable:** detectors only read the fight and draw no random numbers. A seed 7 pilot fight traced the same with and without its fight log and detectors, and the stage check's repeatability test now runs one of its two copies with its fight log, so a detector that changed fights would be caught.
  - **Batch report:** a Review section, after Fights worth watching, gives each detector's rate per minute by combination, beside the designer's from their fight logs that have detectors, then up to three flagged moments per detector from different fights, each with what the detector saw and the command to replay it.
  - **First batch:** 40 average pilot fights with the Designer build. Per minute: drones lost in a burst 0.83, touched a Goo 0.42, flew through a group 0.40, indecision 0.29, idle 0.27, closed in on a near enemy 0.01, creeping and fighting without drones 0. The designer's rates come once they play with detectors on, since their earlier logs have none.

- **Maps (stage 2):**
  - **Recording:** `ReviewRecorder.cs`, run with the detectors, records the track (position, what the VNP was doing, and speed, every `TrackSeconds`), events (hits taken, drones lost, level-ups, detector firings) and snapshots every `StripSpacing` of everything within `StripView` of the VNP: enemies with type and size, drones, material, enemy shots. It keeps the last `StripBefore` snapshots, and when a detector fires saves them with the next `StripAfter` as a strip, up to `MaxStripsPerFight`. All of it goes in the fight log's `maps` section. What the VNP was doing is judged from what can be seen: harvesting while its beam works, fighting with an enemy within `FightReach`, moving away when also moving away from it at `MovingAwaySpeed`, otherwise travelling. Small read-only position accessors were added to the drones and projectiles for it.
  - **Drawing:** `MapDrawer.cs` draws PNGs from a fight log with Godot's `Image`, headless, with a small built-in pixel font for labels. Overview maps are 1000 pixels wide: the path coloured by activity, the gateway, start and end, events (hits sized by damage, drones lost, level-ups, detector firings lettered), the fight time every 30 seconds, and health, drones, level and speed over time with the time limit and the win marked. Strip maps are rows of 320-pixel panels a second apart, centred on the VNP, with its last 4 seconds of path and heading; the panel at the moment has a yellow border.
  - **When maps are drawn:** the designer's fights draw their overview and every strip when the log is written. At the designer's request each of their fights has a folder of its own in `playtests/human/`, holding its log and its maps (`overview.png`, `strip0_...png`), so they stay together; pilot fights logged by the scenario harness to `playtests/pilot/` do the same. Batches keep all their logs in `logs/` and the few maps they draw in `maps/`, since a folder per fight would mostly hold a lone log. Scripts that read the designer's logs look in each fight's folder, and in `playtests/human/` itself for logs from before. The scenario harness draws them with `maps=yes`, and `strip=T1,T2` saves strips around chosen fight times too, so any moment of a pilot fight can be drawn by replaying it. `draw_maps.tscn` draws any log's maps afterwards. A batch draws overviews of each combination's best, worst and typical fight and a few strips per detector (`OverviewsPerCombination`, `StripsPerDetector`), in one run of the drawer, and the report's Review section links them.
  - **Ready for many enemy types and detectors:** the log records each enemy type's name, colour and traits (`EnemySwarm.DefinitionOf`, read-only), and the drawer takes trait markings, each strip's key of types and the detectors' names and letters from the log, with no lists of its own. The Goo-touch detector was renamed "Touched a contact enemy" (`touched_by_contact`), since it fires on any enemy that damages by touch; reports count a detector missing from an older log as zero.
  - **Map colours of their own:** at first maps drew each type in its game colour. When the designer made every Grey Goo type the same dark grey (phase 4), the types could no longer be told apart on strips, and the grey was hard to see on a map's dark background. So the drawer now gives each type a bright colour from a fixed palette, by its place in the log's list of types, whatever its game colour. The palette leaves out colours maps already use (the hazards' yellow and pink, enemy shots' orange, the drones' cyan, the VNP's white, asteroids' grey and debris' brown), and repeats lighter if there are more types than colours. A type's map colour can differ from one log to the next if the list of types changes.
  - **No hitch at the end of a fight:** the designer noticed a hitch when the fight was won and again when the endless run ended. Writing the log and drawing every map on the main thread took over a second in a long fight. Now the main thread only gathers the log, copying every list that keeps growing so an endless run carrying on can't change it, and turning it into text, writing it and drawing the maps happen on a background thread, one write after another. Drawing reads only the log, never Godot's resources, so it is safe off the main thread. Leaving a fight or quitting waits for any write still running. The first write of a fight also spent about 70 ms compiling the log's code, so the log is built once and thrown away as the fight is set up, while the starting weapon menu is open. The main thread's share of a write fell from over a second to about 5 ms. The scenario prints a `FIGHT LOG:` line with both times.
  - **Recording the designer's fights:** the designer reviewed a fight where two Goos ate every drone at clock +0:56. The log showed 32 drones falling to 1 between +0:51 and +0:54, but no strip of it: the burst detector had fired at +0:44 and counted the whole collapse as that one moment, and a designer's fight can't be replayed to draw it. So, at the designer's request:
    - **The whole fight is recorded:** the designer's fights save the picture a strip shows (everything within its view of the VNP) every `RecordSpacing` seconds all fight, into `<log name>.recording.json.gz` beside the log, gzip-compressed; the log names it under `maps`. At first that was every half second; the designer asked for finer detail, so it is now ten times a second, matching the recording of their movement, and each picture holds more: each drone's health and the enemy it is attacking, each enemy's heading, ID and how fast it drains drones, and the shards, mines and missiles the VNP's weapons have out, with the radius each acts over. A 5¼-minute fight came to about 560 KB compressed (3.1 MB uncompressed), and recording cost no measurable frame time (0.41 ms of game logic a frame against 0.40). Strips draw drones redder as they lose health, with a faint line to the enemy each is attacking, and the weapons' objects in their weapon's colour (a mine with the ring of its blast); the key is on two lines above the pictures. `RecordPlayerFights` turns it off. Pilot fights replay exactly, so they aren't recorded, except with the harness's `record=yes`, for testing.
    - **Maps on request:** the designer's fights no longer draw maps when the log is written (`DrawPlayerMaps` turns that back on). `tools\maps.cmd` draws them when wanted, for a fight's folder or `latest`: with nothing else, its overview and every flagged moment; with `strip=T`, `T:S`, `FROM-TO` or `FROM-TO:S` (as the harness's `strip=` takes them), strips of any moments or stretches from the recording, such as `tools\maps.cmd latest strip=356` for clock +0:56. `overview=yes` and `flagged=` add those. The pictures sit on the recording's grid, so the one highlighted as the moment can be up to half of `RecordSpacing` from the time asked for. The maps go in the fight's folder. Like the harness, the drawer stops with exit code 2 on an argument it can't use.
    - **Bursts that worsen fire again:** the burst detector fires again, with a strip of its own, each time the drones lost within `BurstSeconds` reach `BurstWorsens` times the count that last fired it, so a long collapse that started small also gets a strip of its worst part. In a replay of seed 30 it fired at 6 drones and again at 12 ("worsening") six seconds later.
  - **Exact moments:** at first a strip's pictures came only from the snapshots taken every `StripSpacing` on the fight's own clock, so the picture marked as the moment could be up to that long before it, and a strip running into the end of a fight stopped short of it, since the fight pauses for the result screen in the frame the VNP is destroyed. Reviewing the end of a pilot fight (seed 30), Claude's last picture was 0.7 seconds before the fatal Goo touch, so the touch itself couldn't be seen. Now a detector's strip adds a picture at the moment it fired; the fight log adds a picture of the VNP's destruction to every strip still being taken; and a strip asked for with `strip=` is taken on its own schedule. Each of its entries can be `T` (a moment, with `StripBefore` pictures before and `StripAfter` after, one exactly at it), `T:S` (pictures S seconds apart, such as `363:0.25`), `FROM-TO` (a stretch, with no picture highlighted) or `FROM-TO:S`, up to 48 pictures. Its spacing is in the log, and each picture is labelled with its offset to a hundredth of a second. Seed 30 replayed with these strips played the same fight.
  - **Log size:** the track and strips made indented logs about 700 KB; pilot and batch logs are now written compact, about 170 KB, while the designer's stay indented for reading.
  - **Checked:** the stage check's headless fight now draws maps with `maps=yes strip=20` and fails if no overview or strip appears. Drawing a fight's maps takes under a second.
  - **First look:** seed 7's overview showed, without anyone's notes, a pilot spending most of the fight travelling on wide loops far from the gateway, with short stretches of harvesting and constant stop-start in its speed, and reaching level 10 by 4:32. A strip at clock 4:06 showed it heading straight into a pack of basic enemies sitting on its own drones.

- **First review (stage 3):** 90 fights (novice, average and expert pilots, Designer build, random starting weapons), against the designer's one fight with a track so far. Claude looked at the overview maps of the average pilot's typical fight and the designer's fight, measured what they suggested across all 90 fights, replayed seed 21 with `explain`, and drew strips of its drone collapse with `strip=`.
  - **Pilots lose their drones and never rebuild them (confirmed):** in 63 to 90% of pilot fights the drones fall to 2 or fewer, and only 3 to 10% get back to 10; pilots spend 22 to 30% of the fight after 1:00 with 2 or fewer drones, the designer 1%. The cause, from seed 21's reasoning: once regrouping, the pilot backs off until no enemy is within its threat range, which late in a fight never happens, so it spent 64% of its decisions heading for material and 8% harvesting it.
  - **Drones fight packs without the VNP (confirmed, with one designer fight to compare):** seed 21's strip at clock 2:30 shows the drones fighting a pack of basic enemies 10 to 15 units away while the VNP zigzags away, neither committing nor pulling them out; drones attack anything within their engage range of the VNP, so a half-retreat leaves them alone, and 22 drones fell to 13 in five seconds. Pilots lose 36% of their drones while travelling or moving away, the designer 25%; the designer loses most while fighting alongside them (41%).
  - **Pilots travel more and fight and stand still less:** travelling 51% of the time against the designer's 37%, fighting 9% against 20%, standing still 11% against 27%. The designer moves steadily outward, working each cluster in a tight curl before moving on; pilots go back over ground already covered about twice as often (9 to 10% of the time against 4%), a smaller difference than the maps first suggested.
  - **About the maps:** the batch's strips all came from novice fights, since moments were picked in batch order; they should be spread across combinations. The overview maps made whole-fight differences obvious at a glance; strips showed what happened at a moment; `explain` gave the cause.
  - **Suggested detectors:** drones fighting while the VNP is away from them; drones not rebuilt (2 or fewer for a while). Both added: "Drones left fighting" (at least `LeftFightingDrones` drones, an enemy within their engage range but more than `LeftFightingDistance` from the VNP, which moves away from it) and "Drones not rebuilt" (`NotRebuiltDrones` or fewer for `NotRebuiltSeconds`, after the first minute). Batch strips are now taken from each combination in turn.
- **Fixes from the first review, and what they showed:** with the designer's agreement, regrouping backs off only as its pressure says rather than until no enemy is within `RegroupClearRange`, and with drones that matter, backing off is all or nothing (`CommitOrWithdraw`): stay with the drones, or withdraw until no enemy is within their reach plus `WithdrawMargin`, for at most `WithdrawMaxSeconds`. Over 25 expert fights each against the old behavior (variant `old_regroup_and_backoff`), pilots lasted longer (destroyed at a median 5:11 instead of 4:38, 14 alive at 5:00 instead of 11), but drones still collapsed in 92% of fights, unchanged. Measuring why: the designer loses drones faster than pilots (28 to 29 a minute against 21 to 22) and keeps them by building faster (27 to 32 a minute against 19 to 20), from three to five times the experience from debris (4,000 to 5,800 a minute against about 1,200), while asteroid experience is the same. So the gap is staying where fights happen and harvesting their debris, not care with drones; some earlier fixes that keep pilots away from material near enemies work against it.
- **Debris and drones, tested and dropped:** after the designer described staying to collect debris when they can harvest it fast, and going back to asteroids with no drones, two changes were tried on the expert, 25 fights each with and without: being bolder about danger near debris the more harvesting power it has, with debris worth less with few drones; and preferring material its drones can already reach, so it stays while they work. Neither changed drones built, debris collected or drone collapse, so both were taken back out.
- **Compare at the same fight time, not per minute of fight:** measured per minute over whole fights, the designer seemed to build drones and collect debris far faster. Aligned by fight time, pilots and the designer killed at the same pace and held about as many drones up to 2:30 (kills at 2:30: pilots 94, designer 100 and 128; drones: pilots 15, designer 23 and 13). The designer's higher rates came from surviving long into endless mode, where kills come fastest and Debris reward upgrades multiply them. So the real difference is late in the fight, as difficulty climbs, where the designer keeps going and pilots collapse; and comparisons should be made at the same fight times. With two designer fights that have timelines, there is too little to say more.
- **Wave timing, decided:** the designer weighs how long they have before the next wave when deciding, after a fight, whether to stay and harvest debris or go to an asteroid cluster. Pilots may do the same by estimating the next wave from the waves they have seen this fight, knowing nothing until the second; they are not given the schedule (the designer's choice among three options; see the automated playtesting spec's Pilots section). Planned for stage 4, but not built: pilots don't track waves yet. Deferred at the designer's agreement, since calibration has stopped and the designer is satisfied with the pilots for now; it matters again if calibration resumes, or when pilots fly rival and escort VNPs. Waves are now in the fight logs, so it can be compared with the designer's play when built.
- **Automatic calibration (stage 4):** built as `tools/calibration/` (see `tools/README.md`), and run unattended on a `calibration/` branch under the rules in CLAUDE.md, with every step recorded in `docs/dev/calibration/JOURNAL.md`.
  - **The measure:** `compare_to_designer.py` compares a batch's expert fights with the designer's at the same fight times (health, drones, kills and level each minute, and how many are still alive), and gives one number, the gap: the average difference as a fraction of the designer's value. Smaller is closer. `--since` picks the designer's fights by time and `--same-tuning` by tuning fingerprint; `--variant` scores one variant of a comparison batch.
  - **The search:** `calibrate_pilot.py` tries each of the expert's main settings raised and lowered, as tuning variants on the same 50 seeds, keeps a change only if it lowers the gap by at least `--min-gain` there and also wins on 50 separate seeds (from `--confirm-seed`), writes kept changes to `expert.tres`, halves its steps after a pass with nothing kept, and logs every candidate to `playtests/calibration/`. Batches gained `--first-seed` for the second set.
  - **Results:** against the designer's three fights then on the current tuning, two changes were kept: StrengthPerDrone 0.08 to 0.14 (by hand, chunk 2) and HarvestAtCap 0.8 to 0.9 (by the search), taking the gap from 0.165 to 0.120. Then the search found nothing more in three passes, and the one behavior it could test as a setting (no regrouping) was worse, so the run stopped by its own rule: what remained (drones at 3:00) needed a new behavior, not a setting. Later pilot changes (seeing contact enemies coming) made pilots outlive the designer's fights, which raised the gap; with three designer fights as the target, that says more about the target's size than the pilots.
  - **Not built:** detector rates as targets. Deferred at the designer's agreement, since calibration has stopped; when it resumes, the detectors to target should be chosen rather than all of them, since some measure style rather than faults (the indecision detector fires on the designer's deliberate weaving).
- **Iterating on the expert first:** at the designer's suggestion, pilot behavior is improved on the expert alone, aiming at how the designer plays rather than at perfect play, and the average and novice levels are made from it afterwards by turning its settings down. Behavior fixes apply to all levels, since they share the code; only batches change, running one pilot instead of three. Not done yet: average and novice keep their older settings (the expert's calibrated StrengthPerDrone and HarvestAtCap aren't in them), so batches now default to the expert (`--pilots`); making the other levels from it is deferred until skill levels matter, such as for difficulty or rival VNPs.

- **Nothing left out of recordings:** at the designer's request, four guards keep new content in the recordings:
  - **Required by the build:** `CollectPlaced` is abstract on `VnpWeapon` and on Drone Weapons' `DroneAttack` (`weapons/VnpWeapon.cs`, `drone_weapons/DroneAttack.cs`), with each weapon's version beside its code, so a new weapon doesn't compile until it says what it has out in the world: points (shards, mines, missiles), rings (Shockwave) or lines (the Pulse Laser's beam, Beam drones' beams), in `PlacedObject`. A weapon with nothing of its own writes an empty body saying why (Scatter Shot and Projectile drones, whose shots go through the projectiles; Ram, which is the drones themselves).
  - **Shared systems recorded whole:** every shot in the fight's projectiles is recorded, the VNP's and drones' as well as enemies', each marked with the weapon that fired it, so any weapon that fires through them is covered with no code of its own. Strips draw the VNP's and drones' shots thinner, in their weapon's colour.
  - **Mods record themselves:** each picture lists the loadout, each VNP Weapon with its mods and the Drone Weapon in use, read from the weapons' features; strips show it in their key.
  - **A checklist step and a test:** the add-content skill has a step for each kind of content, and the stage check plays every starting weapon recorded and fails if one leaves nothing in its recording.
- **From the first recorded fight's review:** reviewing the designer's first recorded fight (Orbital Shards with harvesting, Beam drones, Rich) turned up three gaps, fixed at the designer's request:
  - **Harvesting by shards counted as travelling:** the VNP was judged to be harvesting only while its own beam worked, so a shard build harvesting on the move looked like travelling on the overview. Harvesting now marks whether the VNP itself did it (`Harvest(..., byVnp)`, passed by its beam and by Orbital Shards), and the VNP counts as harvesting while its beam works or for `HarvestingWithin` seconds after it or a weapon last harvested. The overview and the detectors that leave harvesting out (creeping) use this. In a test fight with shard harvesting, 64% of the fight now shows as harvesting.
  - **The killing hit was missing:** the fight pauses for the result screen in the frame the VNP is destroyed, so the recorder never logged that frame's changes. When the fight ends, the last frame's hit and drones lost are now logged too; in a test fight the logged hits then added up exactly to the damage taken.
  - **The designer's logs had doubled in size,** to about 1.8 MB, mostly the flagged moments' strips saved with every new detail. A recorded fight's log now keeps only each strip's reason and time (`fromRecording`), and `tools\maps.cmd` draws its pictures from the recording at the strips' usual spacing, before and after (`stripBefore`, `stripAfter` in the log). Logs without a recording, such as batch fights, keep their strips' pictures as before.
- **Waves and escalation steps:** at the designer's request, the log's events include each wave as it arrives (`wave`, numbered, with how many groups it sent) and each escalation step past the time limit (`escalation`), so a review can tell what arrived when. The overview marks each on the path, W1, W2 and so on in orange and E1, E2 in yellow, and as dashed lines on the time charts; in a strip, the picture during which one happens names it, such as "WAVE 5 ARRIVES, 9 GROUPS". A wave's groups spawn off screen, so they reach a strip's view a few seconds later. The spawner exposes the last wave's size for this (`EnemySwarm.LastWaveGroups`, inside `#if RECORDING`).
- **Verified in full:** four recorded pilot fights with different setups, covering every VNP Weapon, Drone Weapons, both mods (blocking and harvesting) and all three asteroid amounts, each played into escalation, were checked field by field: pictures every 0.1 s from start to end with no gaps; every part of every picture in full; each weapon shown by its objects or shots; drones' health and targets (over 97% of targets found among the enemies in the picture; the rest were out of view); drain on Goos only; enemy headings; asteroids and debris; mods in the loadout; and the log's hits, drones lost, level-ups, waves and escalation steps adding up to the fight's own statistics. All passed. The check found drones that died that frame recorded with health below zero; they are now recorded at 0.
- **Checked before each commit:** at the designer's request, that field-by-field check is now part of the stage check, run with `--full` before every commit rather than on every check (`check_recording_full.py` in the check-stage skill; CLAUDE.md asks for it). It works out its fights from what exists: VNP Weapons two to a fight, Drone Weapons and asteroid amounts in turn, and each mod in a fight with its weapon, so new content is covered with no change. Since phase 4 stopped the basic enemy spawning, which left no enemy shots to check, each fight also starts with two groups of every enemy type, so every type and its shots are recorded whatever the fight spawns. Four fights cover the game now, in about half a minute.
- **Out of exported builds:** at the designer's request, the hooks on game classes that only recordings read (every `CollectPlaced`, `PlacedObject`, the accessors for drones' health and targets and for what fired a shot, and the earlier `EnemySwarm.DefinitionOf` and `VnpHarvester.Beaming`; after an audit of every public member of the game code used only from `scripts/cheats/`, also `Projectiles.PositionOf`, `VelocityOf` and `IsEnemyShot`, and `EnemySwarm.CountByType`, which the fight log uses. Left unwrapped on purpose: `FightStats.KillsByEnemy`, since fight statistics are game code by design, and the `byVnp` flag on `Harvestables.Harvest`, which does nothing in an export) are inside `#if RECORDING`, a compile symbol `VNP.csproj` defines only for the editor's Debug build. Export builds contain none of them (checked in both export DLLs), and the Debug build still refuses a weapon without its `CollectPlaced` (checked: removing Scatter Shot's gave error CS0534). A special build that records fights would define `RECORDING` and keep `scripts/cheats/playtesting/`; it is a separate symbol from `TOOLS` for that reason.
- **What a pilot perceives, on strips:** since the pilot rebuild's stage 1 (`docs/dev/PILOT_SPEC.md`), each recorded picture also holds the hazards enemies and shots declare and the zones weapons declare, and strips draw them (yellow hurts the VNP, pink drones, dim not yet landed, with where each moves in the next second; faint rings for weapons' reach). A review sees exactly what a pilot reads, and a new attack shows with no code in the drawer.
- **Weapon effects on maps:** things a weapon has out in the world are drawn on strips through a small generic hook on every VNP Weapon, `CollectPlaced`, which adds each object's position and the radius it acts over. Orbital Shards add their shards, Mine Layer its mines and Seeker Missiles their missiles; a weapon that only fires beams or the fight's own shots adds nothing. The log names each weapon and its colour, as it does enemy types, so a new weapon appears on maps with no change to the drawer.

## Suggested build order

Build one stage at a time, and stop after each so the designer can try it.

1. **Detectors in fight logs,** for pilots and the designer, with counts, rates and flagged moments, and the batch report's rates beside the designer's.
2. **Overview and strip maps:** the track and snapshots in fight logs, overview maps, strips of flagged moments, strips of any moment of a pilot fight from the scenario harness, and the report listing them.
3. **Claude's review of a batch:** the first reviews, reported to the designer, with what was confirmed and any new detectors they suggest.
4. **Automatic calibration,** with the detector rates among its targets.

All four stages are built. Phase 3 resumed after this spec, with its stages 3 and 4. Each stage here is finished when it does what its line describes, the check-stage skill passes, and the designer has tried it.

## Out of scope

- A replay page the designer can scrub through. Possible later from the same tracks and snapshots.
- Judging how the fight feels or looks, which stays with the designer.
- Machine learning, as in the automated playtesting spec.
